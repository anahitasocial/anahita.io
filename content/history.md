The exact start date of the Anahita project is unknown. The idea emerged in
the mid-2000s, while Rastin Mehr, Anahita's founder and core architect, 
was a building online learning tools in their first job out of school 
and contributing to the open-source [Joomla!](https://www.joomla.org) CMS in their free time. 

They dreamed of an open-source platform for sharing knowledge and connecting people, and it led them to
leave their job and work full-time on their own software company, [rmd studio](https://www.rmdstudio.com),
and on what would become the Anahita social networking platform and
framework.

A few months later, Rastin met Ash at a Facebook Developers event, and they
became business associates and collaborators on Anahita for the next six years,
including their time at Mozilla's WebFWD incubator. It was a formative time.
With the help and guidance of Johan Janssens, co-founder of Joomla! and
founder of Timble, who was Anahita's mentor until 2017, they developed many of
the core ideas of Anahita's design: its graph architecture, a privacy system
built on the social graph, and different kinds of nodes, such as actors and
media. They first built Anahita as a PHP extension of the Joomla! CMS, but
after a few releases they forked Joomla! to fit Anahita's architecture better.

After Ash's departure in 2013, Rastin went on maintaining and reshaping the
codebase until it became a standalone client-server platform and framework for
building projects on a social networking infrastructure.

During the Covid-19 pandemic, Rastin experimented with microservices, and in
2023 they decided to reimagine and rebuild Anahita from the ground up in Go, as
a collection of services, true to their original idea of a real graph
architecture, now for the decentralised web and the fediverse. The result is
the codebase we have today.

Anahita is named after the Persian goddess of the waters, of love, fertility
and wisdom. Its first public instance, Banu.io, is on its way. Banu means a
lady of high standing.
